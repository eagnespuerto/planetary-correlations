Read with care

  • Selection bias. These are known planets, not all planets. Transit surveys favour big planets on short orbits, radial-velocity surveys favour massive ones, and most host stars were never searched thoroughly. A low percentage usually means "not looked for hard enough" rather than "rare".
  • Estimated values. The archive's composite table fills in missing radius or mass with a mass–radius relation, so planet types for planets known only by mass (or only by radius) are approximate.
  • Spectral classes. On this page, star types come from colour (Gaia BP−RP) in the Gaia section and from effective temperature in the archive sections. Neither is a spectroscopic classification. The main-sequence cut uses a simple colour–magnitude band, so some subgiants and unresolved binaries leak in.
  • Planet types by radius: Earth-size < 1.25 R⊕, super-Earth 1.25–2, sub-Neptune 2–4, Neptune-like 4–8, gas giant ≥ 8. Hot Jupiter: radius ≥ 8 R⊕ and period < 10 days. Temperate: radius < 2 R⊕ and 0.35–1.75 × Earth's insolation.
